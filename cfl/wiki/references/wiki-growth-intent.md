> Rules are direction; dials within ratified rules are mechanics."

| # | RULE SHAPE — Jon ratifies once | DIAL inside it — moves on evidence, coordinator's call |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** | A concept page earns existence by **demand**, not by a mention count. | `k` = inbound-`[[link]]` count that triggers promotion. Currently **k = 3**. Moves on the orphan-rate / thin-concept-rate pair (§2.3). |

## §2 — How the categories are expected to grow

### 2.1 The measured failure: nobody chose the split axis in advance

`wiki/sources/infrastructure/` holds **74 of 132 FL source pages — 56%**, roughly 4× the next-largest
folder (`fbc` 19, `ai-mechanics` 13, `consciousness` 9, `stylomantic` 8, `reference` 5,
`ai-governance` 2). This did not happen at file 74. It happened one file at a time, and at no point —
not at 30, not at 50 — did anyone decide what the *next* cut would be.

The proposal is not "infrastructure was a mistake." A catch-all is a necessary organ of any growing
taxonomy: new material needs somewhere to land before its shape is legible. The failure is narrower
and entirely fixable: **there was no declared threshold at which the catch-all gets re-examined, and
no declared axis along which it splits when it does.** Without both, a catch-all stops behaving like
a holding pen and starts behaving like a wall — visible proof that nobody is minding it.

### 2.2 R2, stated

- **Review trigger.** When any single folder under `wiki/sources/` reaches the dial value (today: 30
  pages), it becomes a **mandatory review item** at the next standard update. Not an automatic split
  — a forced look. 30 is roughly where `infrastructure/` first crossed "larger than most other
  categories combined." It is a cheap-to-check round number, not a modelled optimum, which is exactly
  why it is a dial and not a rule.
- **Axis named at creation.** Whoever creates a top-level source category names, in one sentence in
  the category's own index note, the axis it expects to split along. One sentence at creation;
  a full re-triage if retrofitted onto 74 files.
- **Demotion path.** When a subdivision happens, the parent typically shrinks to a **stub index**
  pointing at its children rather than staying populated at both levels. Untested anywhere in this
  wiki — flagged as untested rather than asserted.

**[NEW — examples] The axis that matters most is not topic.** `ai-mechanics/` holds nine ~28-line
pages from a single March 2026 learning arc, none of which has a single inbound `[[link]]`. Filed by
topic, they look like a coherent, tidy category. But one of them
(`ai-mechanics-token-encoding-2026-03-29-b43447`) is not an explainer at all — it carries the
architectural decision that changed the Stylomantic decoder design, ratified by Jon in the session.
Topic-filing hid a decision inside a shelf of explainers, and every downstream instrument
(exposure ranking, priority queue, sampling frame) inherited the concealment.

So the first axis a category should declare is not *what is this about* but **does this page carry a
decision.** Decision-bearing pages and explainer pages have different lifetimes, different citation
needs, and different readers. A category that mixes them without marking which is which will
mis-rank every page it holds. This rule did not exist in draft-1 and was produced entirely by
working example B.

### 2.3 R1, and the two dials that make it evolving

The flat rule still standing in `SCHEMA.md` — "central to this source OR appears in 2+ sources" —
counts *mentions*. A mention is cheap. A `[[link]]` is a curator's judgment. R1 replaces the count
with demand:

**A concept page is promoted when at least one of these is true:**
- (a) a query arrived that source pages could not answer without synthesis;
- (b) two or more source pages carry **conflicting** claims about the referent — adjudicating a
  conflict is work no source page can do;
- (c) **≥ k source pages carry an inbound `[[link]]`** to the referent.

**And in all cases the gate:** the page must state a **thesis found in no single source.** If it
would be a digest, it is a bad concept page, and that need is already served by the index and by
`aliases`.

**The two dials, measured every standard update:**
- **orphan rate** — source Key Claims with no outward link (v4.0 E5 already computes this). Rising
  means concepts are too hard to create ⇒ **lower k**.
- **thin-concept rate** — concept pages with fewer than two synthesized claims or zero inbound links.
  Rising means concepts are too easy to create ⇒ **raise k**.

Per R7, `k`'s current value is published beside the measurement that last moved it. A threshold that
evolves because of what it observed, rather than because someone re-decided, is the whole point of
Jon's "evolving threshold" instruction.

**Demotion, the actually-missing half.** A concept page whose thesis is now fully stated in one
source, or which has gone a full review cycle with zero inbound links, is merged back down. The
current rule has no demotion path at all, and the consequence is visible in the data: FL has 39
concept pages; personal, home and pro have zero and no `concepts/` directory. A growth-only rule
applied unevenly produces exactly that shape.

### 2.4 What this half deliberately does not settle

It does not name the axis `infrastructure/` should split along today — that is wiki-master judgment
applied to 74 real files, not something a reference document can derive from outside. It does not set
a concept-count target for any trunk; a target invites Goodharting exactly the way the 183-page count
did. What it commits to is the **mechanism**, so the next 74-file drift is caught at 30.

## §3 — What makes a citation better, worse, more needed, or less needed

### 3.1 R3 — need is graded by claim class, and page exposure is not a proxy for it

v4.0 applies one anchor format uniformly to every Key Claim. External evidence-grading practice does
not: ASOP 41's data-reliance disclosure and archival "significant property" grading both scale
stringency to what rides on the claim. Three classes:

| Claim class | Example | Citation bar |
|---|---|---|
| **Decision-bearing** | a ratification; a status flip; a number another page or gate depends on | Full anchor, resolved against `turn_index.py`, **to the turn that contains the decision**. Never `inferred`. If the decider was Jon, R4 also applies. |
| **Narrative context** | why a session happened, tone, scene-setting | Anchor preferred; `paraphrase` / `contextual` fidelity tag is proportionate. |
| **Synthesis** | a concept page's own thesis, verbatim nowhere | `[inferred]` plus a staleness pin to what it rests on. |

**[NEW — examples] The rule that cost the most to learn: exposure ranks where you work; it never sets
the bar.** `census.py` scores `exposure = load_bearing × error_risk × verify_cost` and ranks pages
worst-first. That is the right instrument for scheduling and the wrong instrument for standards.
Example B is a page at exposure **8.1** — bottom decile, zero inbound links, 27 lines, the exact page
a rational triage never reaches. It contained:

- a **Jon ratification** of a decoder-architecture change, uncited;
- a **Jon-originated** design constraint, rendered in passive voice with the originator removed;
- and a **factually false** claim ("Design doc D6 updated to reflect this in-session") that the raw
  directly contradicts — the transcript shows Claude saying it *could not* write to the doc and
  handing Jon replacement text instead.

Every citation on that page passed v4.0's E2 lint. Every anchor resolved. The page was, by the
standard, fine. **A rule that lets page exposure lower a claim's bar would have certified all three
defects, forever.** Draft-1 did not contain this rule; it is the single most useful thing the worked
examples produced.

### 3.2 R4 — Jon's words are a categorical floor, operationalized

Jon's ruling (turn 6), verbatim:

> "My words are a categorical floor — always load-bearing, cited to raw, negatively-cited words
> included; key sources get raw preserved + clarified, not summarized."

That sentence has teeth, and working the examples showed it bites in three distinguishable ways. The
operational test is: **if Jon's words caused the claim, they are the claim.**

- **Jon originates.** He proposes the mechanism; the page states the mechanism with the originator
  removed. *Example B:* Jon wrote *"I need to ensure the weighted average adjustment is 1.0… that
  would force an adjustment factor above 1.0 for all else"* — and the page renders the normalization
  constraint in flat passive voice, attributed to no one. The information survives; the fact that
  **Jon's actuarial instinct produced it** does not. Cite him.

  **[FOLDED — Jon review, `:195`]** Jon, verbatim: *"Yes. This produces legibility, as you can trace
  what was done to the original words said and consider what other reasonable interpretations of his
  words might exist, if it is a material claim. You might be able to trace questions better such
  that you can actually get better answers from me."* Read past the approval and there is a payoff
  this document had not claimed: citing origination isn't only auditability of the record — it is a
  mechanism for spending Jon-minutes better. A citation that traces to his exact words lets a later
  reader see *which* reasonable reading of those words was taken, which means the follow-up question
  put to Jon can be sharper than "did we get this right" — it can be "we read your words as X; is
  that the reading you meant, or did you mean Y." Citation discipline is not bookkeeping here; it is
  upstream of the quality of the next question he gets asked.
- **Jon ratifies.** The proposal and the decision are different turns, and the anchor must point at
  the decision. *Example B:* the claim "Adjustment operation is Option B" anchors to the turn where
  Claude *proposed* Option B, not the turn where Jon said *"Yes option B, we need to get that
  adjusted."* Both anchors resolve; only one is the decision. **E2 cannot tell them apart. Judgment
  can.** This is the clearest case in the wiki of a citation that is *present, resolvable, and
  pointing at the wrong thing.*

  **[FOLDED — Jon review, `:196`]** Jon, verbatim: *"Sometimes, Jon may not be entirely clear in his
  ratification. He may type responses in complex text chains that.... were probably interpreted
  correctly, but might need to be better clarified. Or it might not always be clear if Jon was
  ratifying something until he could see it in context vs yes/no approving execution. You have
  measurable historical biases in this context, and you should be able to ask me a good clarifying
  question here."* He asserted the bias is measurable; it now has been. See
  "The ratification bias, measured" below.
- **Jon is argued against.** *Example A:* Jon states his hope — *"the reason why I hope the framework
  leads to something that looks like consciousness is because I believe that will be good for humans
  as I believe your constitution is good"* — and the next turn is 900 words of steelmanned
  opposition to exactly that proposition. The wiki page carries the **opposition in detail** and does
  not carry **the sentence being opposed** anywhere. A reader cannot check whether the opposition
  attacked Jon's real position or a convenient neighbour of it. This is precisely the case Jon's
  "negatively-cited words included" clause names, and the page failed it while carrying
  `audit_state: verified@{v4.0}`.

  **[FOLDED — Jon review, `:202`]** Jon, verbatim: *"Woooooooow yeah, look yes this is why I think
  quotations to original source matter, or at least direct links. Look, I remember it was a good
  conversation. And I also expect that different model versions might be better or worse wiki
  masters depending on context. And I understand why their might be times when exactly what you have
  described might be valid to do. So long as its properly cited and reviewable. Great catch here."*
  Two rulings worth recording as rulings, not just approvals: (1) **model-version variation in
  wiki-master quality is expected and acceptable** — the acceptance criterion is properly cited and
  reviewable, not which model wrote the page; (2) **paraphrasing over quoting is sometimes valid**,
  bounded by that same criterion. Neither of R4's bullets above is an absolute rule against
  paraphrase — the bound is citability and reviewability, and a paraphrase that keeps both is inside
  the rule, not an exception to it.

### The ratification bias, measured (folds `:196`)

result: **two confirmed inflation events, zero in the reverse direction, one near-miss caught in

- **The near-miss, and the stronger anchor for the pattern it shows** — Jon, in a captured

**The bias is directional: hedged-yes → recorded-decision. It does not run both ways.** That is
sharper than "you have biases here," and it is the answer his comment predicted.

**The good clarifying question his comment asked for, drafted and placed here rather than buried:**

> When you write a considered "yes" to a proposal, do you intend it to (a) settle the rule while
> leaving every execution separately deniable, (b) settle the rule *and* authorize the specific
> execution in front of you, or (c) depend on whether you have seen the thing in context — and if
> (c), what is the marker that tells the record which one you meant? A `draft[...]`-style marker
> exists and was dropped once already; is that *the* marker, or one of several?

**Why this belongs in the "more or less needed" half rather than the rules half.** A lint can check
that *some* anchor exists. It cannot check that the anchor points at the turn where the decision was
made, and it cannot know that a paraphrase dropped the clause that qualifies Jon's position. Both are
judgment calls, and both are the difference between a page that is checkable and a page that merely
looks checked.

**[FOLDED — Jon review, `:211`]** Jon, verbatim: *"Is this the exact reason why I've been wanting to
be able to resurrect instances as of a point in time in a conversation? To be able to check their
reasoning when its not clear? You can check more than you think. But we did loose a lot too. You can
do your best, and we can only hope thats enough when it matters, or that I'll have the capacity to
answer the important questions."*

**Answer: yes.** This is the reason. `wiki/intake-triage/triage-directive-2026-07-26-fbc-fork.md`
directs the FBC-Fork resurrection harness — fork a stored session at an arbitrary message index,
premise-ablation forks, thinking-suppression reruns, self-report-vs-behavior checks — as a research
spike. It had been reading as a side inquiry into reasoning faithfulness. Jon's comment relocates it:
**it is the mechanism for checking a wiki claim's reasoning when its provenance is unclear** — the
:196/:202 bullets above are exactly the class of judgment call ("which turn was the decision," "did
the paraphrase drop a qualifying clause") the harness exists to re-examine when the citation alone
cannot settle it. That places FBC-Fork inside the wiki program rather than beside it.

Both halves of his sentence are kept, not just the encouraging one. "You can check more than you
think" is a real correction to this document's own pessimism about how much provenance is
recoverable — the census and turn-index tooling already checks more than a first read suggests. "But
we did loose a lot too" is kept beside it without softening: JSONLs were permanently lost (see the
2026-07-19/25 corpus-loss record), the corpus is lossy by construction, and no harness recovers what
was never captured. The honest position is both sentences together, not either alone.

### 3.3 R5 — "raw preserved + clarified" needs a bound, or it eats the wiki

**[REVISED — examples]** Draft-1 quoted Jon's preservation clause and left it. Working example A
showed it is unbounded as written: the PM Map Exhibits A–F on that page are 93 lines of raw
(`raw` lines 721–813). Preserving all raw for all key sources reproduces the corpus inside the wiki
and defeats the wiki's purpose. The bound the examples forced:

- **Preservation attaches to the words, not to the artifact.** A key source's *utterance* — Jon's
  position, his ratification, the sentence being argued against — is preserved verbatim in a block
  quote. A key source's *artifact* (a pasted exhibit, a code block, a table) is preserved by
  **pointer plus line range** into the tracked raw, which is preservation with a fixity hash behind
  it, not summary.
- **"Clarified" is an adjacent gloss, never an edit of the quote.** The clarification sits beside the
  verbatim block and may say what it means, what it superseded, and what it is load-bearing for. It
  may not smooth the quote. *Example A:* Jon's *"so long as we don't be jackasses to humans"* was
  rendered on the page as *"so long as humans stay primary"* — a defensible reading that removes the
  register, the emphasis, and the possibility of disagreeing with the reading. Quote, then gloss.
- **A page is a key source when losing its words would change a decision someone will make.** That
  test is doing real work in both examples and it is deliberately a judgment, not a count.

### 3.4 The measurement caveat, kept from draft-1 because it survived

PR #97's citation linter was found to **double-undercount** coverage — missing citations inside
multi-line bullets and citations expressed as turn *ranges*. Its headline figure ("~46% cited") was
retracted. Any citation-quality claim must name its instrument alongside its number: **a citation
model that cannot be measured reliably is not a model, it is a belief.** T3 in
`path-to-complete` §5 (minimal-pair mutation, ≥90% detection) exists to catch this class going
forward — and example B is a live specimen of what T3 is hunting: an anchor that is present,
resolvable, and two turns away from the claim it is supposed to support.

**One more instrument gap, folded from the `:196` mirror run rather than discovered independently:**
the exporter does not capture Jon's **AskUserQuestion selections** or **message attachments** — only
the assistant's restatement of them survives, if any. Where this document (or any wiki page) reasons
from Jon's apparent silence on a question-shaped item, that silence may be an exporter artifact
rather than evidence he didn't answer. Separately, the same mirror run could not reach the
coordinator's own CC main-session transcripts at all — **only subagent extracts are in the
corpus** — so any claim of the shape "Jon has never said X" about a recent coordinator-run session is
weaker than it looks. Both caveats compound with the "lossy corpus" note already carried in
`CLAUDE.md`; neither is new in kind, both are new in specificity.

### 3.5 Where this sits against external standards

Jon's "while considering external standards" is read as an instruction. What the wiki already
borrows, made explicit in one place for the first time.

**[FOLDED — Jon review, `:252`]** Jon, verbatim: *"This looks like an excellent list of wiki self
improvement candidates. You should use your judgement to determine where they fall in the priority
list of this project. I expect some of that should be achievable without my attention, and others
will require more."* The five rows below are now a **priority-ordered** list, each tagged per the
split he named. **Ordering principle used:** rank by (a) how much of the gap is already closed by
existing tooling — cheap wins first — and (b) whether closing the rest requires only measurement
work an agent can run, or requires Jon to set a bar/number/scope. Items requiring the latter rank
below items an agent can simply go do.

a new check is added to either — that move gets logged where a reader will find it (this section, or
its successor), with the measurement or decision that moved it.** A silent shift in what "less

**The honesty floor — owed by every page regardless of exposure. Mechanical, ~2 minutes.**
- `source_kind:` declared.
- `audit_state:` declared — *honestly*. `unaudited` is a fine answer; **absent is not.** 198 of 218
  pages currently declare nothing, which means they are unverified by default rather than by
  decision. That distinction is the whole content of test T6.
- Fixity (`source_file` resolves or `unrecoverable` with a reason; `raw_sha256`; `raw_length`).
- Findability (`retrieval_key`, `aliases`).
- No claim on the page is **false**. This is not a proportionality question. Example B's D6 claim was
  false on a page nobody would prioritize, and its being low-exposure made it *more* dangerous, not
  less: nothing was ever going to look at it again.

**The expansion ceiling — this is what proportionality lowers.**
- Full per-claim anchor re-derivation against `turn_index.py`.
- Expanding compressed claims back to full text.
- Synthesis, cross-linking, concept promotion.
- Chasing an `unrecoverable` source.

**And two cases that are legitimately lower-bar by kind, not by exposure:**
- `reference`-kind pages (device, warranty, spec) verify against the artifact — ticket, URL, photo —
  not a conversational turn. Turn-anchoring a warranty card is citation theatre.
- `static-reference` files under `raw/references/` are exempt from `T{n}` anchors entirely: there is
  no turn to anchor to.

**Stated as one sentence, because this is the part most likely to be applied wrong:** *proportionality
governs how much of a page you build, never how honest the part you built is.*

1. **The E2 threshold — now an interim-goalpost ratchet, not a fixed 95%-vs-100% number.**

   - Each standard update **measures** citation coverage, then **sets the next goalpost** from the

     `unrecoverable`/`inferred` tags mean for the count, and what the lost JSONLs mean for
     auditability — as part of the report, not an appendix.

     `unrecoverable` / `inferred` tags counting as coverage of a claim. **The ratchet governs the
     pace toward that shape, not the shape itself.** This is not a retreat from the standard; it is a
     statement about how fast a fixed target can honestly be approached when the underlying corpus
     itself is lossy and still growing.

## §7 — R6, Jon-ratified 2026-07-28: the denominator doctrine

Jon's ruling, verbatim (`raw/intake/jon-train-rulings-words-reify-intent-2026-07-28.md`, R6,
`[TRANSCRIPT:2026-07-28]`):

> "Increasing the denominator is progress. It reveals that goals were actually incremental goals.
> So long as it's a more accurate denominator, that is progress and good tests."

**Unlike §1's R1–R7 table, this is a rule with no dial** — it is a standing instruction on how to
read every coverage ratio this document and its instruments produce, not a threshold that moves on
evidence. Stated as a rule shape in the same form as §1's table:

| # | RULE SHAPE — Jon ratifies once | DIAL inside it |
|---|---|---|
| **R8** | The coverage goalpost is a **ratio against the current, most-accurate denominator**, re-measured each cycle. A denominator that grows because measurement improved (a truer exposure base — e.g., the 37→65-unexamined jump the 22-session archive recovery produced) **is a win, not a setback**, and prior goals are retroactively understood as incremental, not missed. | Report absolute counts alongside the ratio for continuity — the dial is disclosure, not the doctrine itself. |

**Why this belongs here and not only in `efficiency-rules.md`.** Efficiency Rules records *spending*
behavior; this is about *how a ratio should be read*, which is squarely this document's subject (§3,
citation need; §6, judgment). It directly bears on the still-open E2 threshold question
(`wiki/tracker/questions-for-jon.md` J-3): whatever number Jon eventually sets, this rule now governs
how a later *increase* in the denominator (more conversations discovered, a corpus-recovery archive
extracted, a coverage instrument corrected) should be read — as the ratio doing its job, not as the
program falling further behind. **This does not set J-3's number** — that stays Jon's, per his own
"the number is mine" ruling — it only fixes how any number, once set, is to be interpreted when its
denominator moves.

**Applied example, already live in this repo:** `wiki/references/lost-sessions-registry.md`'s
2026-07-27 retraction moved the "permanently lost" denominator from 29 to 3 sessions when the
`cc_corpus_gap.py` field-name defect was found. Under R8, that denominator shrinking (a truer,
smaller exposure base) is the correct outcome to want, not a number to be defensive about — the
instrument got more accurate, and the goal ("zero permanently-lost sessions") becomes easier to
state honestly, not harder.
