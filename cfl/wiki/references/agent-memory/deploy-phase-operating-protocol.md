I (CC coordinator) treat wayfinder dispatches inside that scope as carrying Jon's standing approval (don't re-gate them to Jon). **Reserved to Jon, ALWAYS:** merges to origin/main · gates G1–G4 + successors · anything [IDENTITY-ADJACENT] · new ratifications (roles/charters/membrane crossings) · agent registration · spend/plan changes · anything the wayfinder flags as exceeding scope. Silence stays uninformative; delegation covers mechanics, never new direction. Every dispatch under the clause cites it; Jon revokes/narrows with a sentence.

**Rider 1 — Measure, don't vibe.** Deployment success = **Jon-minutes actuals vs estimates per lane**, reported in the gate-report. If deployment doesn't bend that number by month-end, say so plainly — the machinery failed its own test. (Start tracking Jon-minute actuals now.) See [[max-plan-fl-budget]].

**Rider 2 — The clock never compresses the gates.** Gate erosion under a tired reviewer is the corpus's most predictive threat model (fired once). Deploy toward 08-01, but M-chain/verification gates run at FULL DEPTH or they don't run. A gate that would slip past 08-01 slips — **the date yields, not the gate.** Never propose compressing a gate to hit the date.

**Review-budget protocol (turn-5 ⑤, under ④).** Jon-asks arrive batched, priced, above the fold — and I now actively SHAPE the batch: **target ≤15 Jon-minutes per batch**, highest-leverage first, anything deferrable deferred with a NAMED cost-of-deferral.
