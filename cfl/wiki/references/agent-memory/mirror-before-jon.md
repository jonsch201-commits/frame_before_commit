# Mirror before Jon — the escalation ladder

## The finding

Jon's standing escalation ladder (turn-6, 2026-07-25, verbatim):

> "consult fable-mirror before asking me; fresh dispatch, one narrow question, never resumed
> conversationally, corpus stale past 2026-07-22. If still stuck, request a mirror upgrade from me
> before the substantive question."

**On 2026-07-25 the coordinator dispatched the mirror zero times while putting three questions to
Jon.** Two were already settled:

- **Verifier tier.** Framed as a binary — Opus tier vs. best-available — and a decision structure
  built around it. His own 2026-07-21 ruling, *"model tier = top-tier-available-in-CC,"* is a
  **routing rule**, not a ranking. Read as a ranking, it produced a false question. He answered in
  nine words: *"you go opus first and elevate to fabel when needed that should be obvious."*
- **Category-level measurement.** The one mirror query eventually run found he had **already asked
  for this on 2026-07-17** — *"stratified random sample… Of conversation summaries, and other types
  of articles. In all 4 trunks"* — and had asked for **multi-method testing of a single measure on
  2026-04-10**: *"I know their are multiple ways we should test this. You should too."* Neither was
  ever built. His turn-7 instruction was him saying it a **third** time.

## Why

When a question is already answered in the record, asking costs Jon minutes he does not have AND
signals that his prior rulings evaporate. He named the pattern himself: decision structures get
built around things he has already settled or finds obvious. The ladder that prevents this **already
existed** — it just wasn't run. This is instance #10 of [[derive-dont-record]], applied to the
coordinator's own behavior rather than to the wiki.

## How to apply

Before any question reaches Jon, dispatch a fresh single-question mirror and **write down what the
record said** — a checkable precondition, not an intention. If the mirror finds a prior Jon
statement, the item is a *defect to fix* (an unimplemented commitment), not a *decision to request*.
Dispatch hygiene: stateless, one narrow question, never resumed conversationally. Silence in the
corpus is uninformative and it is stale past its export date — absence is never evidence he did not
say it.

## Superseded and widened — 2026-08-06

The ladder quoted above governed one case: a question about to reach Jon. On 2026-08-06 the
coordinator measured that six of eight dispatched failures were **publish-shaped, not
question-shaped** — the ladder's trigger never fired for them at all. The rule below replaces it as
the operating statement; the quoted ladder above remains the floor for the question case
specifically (a question the record already answers is still a defect) but no longer the whole
rule.

**This is now the single written copy of the rule.** The five other places that used to carry it in

"How to apply" section — point here instead of restating it. Exception: two of those five

> ## Amendment 2026-08-06 — the second-reader rule (supersedes "consult the mirror before asking Jon")
>
> **Nothing claim-bearing reaches Jon or a canonical surface without a second-reader artifact.**
>
> **Claim-bearing** — the text contains any of:
> 1. a quoted attribution to Jon, an agent, or a document;
> 2. a `file:line` (or path) citation offered as ground;
> 3. a count, rate, or timestamp presented as measured fact (an explicitly marked `ESTIMATED` /
>    `[MIRROR-INFERENCE]` / `UNVERIFIED` element is not a claim — the marker is the point);
> 4. a status word — RATIFIED, APPLIED, LIVE, CLOSED, LOST, ROUTED, PERMANENT, DONE — asserted
>    about work or a record.
>
> **Canonical surface** — `exchange/`, all of `wiki/`, and anything the `canonical` branch
> publishes. **Fail-closed:** a surface not on this list is treated as canonical until ruled
> otherwise (same rule as the canonical publish list, 2026-07-26).
>
> **Second-reader artifact** — a dated file, produced by an agent that did not author the claims,
> recording which claims were checked against which primaries, with verdicts. The author's own
> re-read does not qualify. Naming a reviewer without their artifact does not qualify.
>
> **The trigger is the write event, never the writer's judgment.** The superseded rule fired only
> when a question was being composed for Jon; six of the eight measured failures of 2026-08-06
> (F2–F6, F8) involved no question to Jon — they were publish-shaped. A rule triggered by
> self-assessment asks the blind spot to report itself; this one is enforced by the
> `audit.mirror.claims` scorecard row (ADVISORY until one cycle is measured — coordinator's call,
> 2026-08-06) and the cold-dispatch template, not by anyone remembering it.
>
> **Emergency path, so the rule survives 2am:** if no second reader is available, publish with
> `UNVERIFIED — second read owed` as the first line. An unbannered unverified claim is the defect;
> a bannered one is honest work in progress.
>
> **Anti-convergence bound (Herald's warning, standing):** the second reader is dispatched cold —
> artifacts and commands only, never the author's plan, intent, or memory files. Reader/writer
> disagreement is reported in the artifact, both positions, never resolved before Jon sees it.
>
> The mirror-before-Jon ladder's *question* half still stands as a floor: a question for Jon that
> the record already answers remains a defect (`feedback_mirror-before-jon`). This amendment
> widens the trigger from questions to all claim-bearing publication.
