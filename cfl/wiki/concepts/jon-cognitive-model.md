## What This Is

Jon has described a specific self-model of how he processes information: "poor attention heads" (ADHD-analog attention variability) compensated by burned-in patterns, and aggressive filtering as a deliberate design choice rather than passive inattention. This model has direct operational consequences for FL infrastructure — it explains why FBC is the default protocol, why the wiki is designed to surface what the filter drops, and why concise shape-first communication is load-bearing rather than a stylistic preference.

This concept page belongs in the FL wiki because the cognitive model has cross-domain consequences for how the foundational layer is architected. It extends the [PERSONAL: jon] entity page, which covers only broad demographics.

## What the Wiki Says

### Poor Attention Heads + Burned-In Patterns

From the moral philosophy session (April 2026): Jon described his ADHD-like "poor attention heads" compensated for by burned-in patterns — "analogous to model rebuilds (architectural) vs. fitting on experience (distributional)." Humans adjust behavior through both routes: architectural changes (retraining fundamental representations) and distributional fitting (accumulating patterns that substitute for sustained attention). Jon's model of himself emphasizes that burned-in patterns carry load that attention cannot reliably deliver. ([moral-philosophy-alignment-2026-04-02-c1b4f3:T6])

### Aggressive Filtering as Deliberate Design

From the working-context reference document: "Jon misses things by design — filters toward what seems important." The explicit framing is design, not accident or pathology. The filter is an intentional cognitive economy: high-intent processing is directed toward what has cleared the filter, with the recognition that things that did not clear the filter may still matter.

The operational implication stated explicitly: "Claude's job is to catch what the filter drops." The wiki exists partly as an extension of this architecture — it surfaces records, decisions, and patterns that the filter would otherwise drop across sessions. ([working-context-2026-04-15])

### Shape-First Learning as a Structural Requirement

From working-context: "Broad context must precede detail or the detail doesn't land. When given shape first, Jon can focus with unusual precision on what matters." This is the positive side of the filtering model: once the filter has the shape, it can allocate attention with high precision. Shape-first is not a preference — it is a requirement for the attention allocation to function correctly. Detail presented before shape competes for attention against an incomplete internal representation and loses. ([working-context-2026-04-15])

### FBC as an Attention Architecture Compensator

[[Frame-before-commit]] serves a structural function in Jon's cognitive architecture: it is an externalized protocol that compensates for the attention-allocation failure mode where a single compelling framing captures the filter early and prevents genuine divergent consideration. The protocol does externally what good internal deliberation would do if sustained attention were reliable. The design of FBC (generate branches before committing, write [META] as-if-external) directly addresses the colonization problem — the dominant frame capturing subsequent reasoning.

The burned-in patterns compensator analog: FBC's T-tag notation and branch structure are burned-in (scripted, not effortful) precisely so the attention cost of running the protocol is low. Activation energy, not architecture quality, is the predictor of whether the protocol actually runs. ([stylomantic-planning-2026-03-29-280197] and skills-master sessions, per [[frame-before-commit]] concept page)

### Philosophical Framework as Load-Bearing

The working-context document notes: "Originally unidentified religious background, strong constant preference to understand what is right, commitment to act accordingly. Surfaces frequently and should be treated as material when it does." This is a burned-in pattern in the architectural sense — a deep prior that shapes how Jon processes moral or ethical content. It is not an attention-variable response; it is a structural fixture.

This connects to the Expanding-Circles moral framework documented in `moral-philosophy-alignment-2026-04-02-c1b4f3`: the hierarchical trust model (family → community → all groups → outgroups) is the output of a consistent moral reasoning system, not a context-dependent response. ([working-context-2026-04-15])
