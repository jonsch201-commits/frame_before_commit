## Key Claims

- **The tight part of the analogy: sleep is genuinely compressive, in the same shape as
  compaction.** Systems consolidation during slow-wave sleep replays hippocampal traces into
  neocortex in a gist-preserving, detail-discarding transfer — Tononi and Cirelli's synaptic
  homeostasis hypothesis has sleep renormalizing synaptic weight globally while preserving
  *relative* strengths (absolute magnitude discarded, structure retained), which the session calls
  "compaction's actual signature." Perturbational complexity (TMS-EEG) drops sharply in slow-wave
  sleep and recovers in REM/waking, so there is a defensible claim that in deep NREM there is
  briefly nothing it is like to be you. [paraphrase] ([sleep-identity-ai-compaction-analogy-2026-07-25-9f61ec:T3], [sleep-identity-ai-compaction-analogy-2026-07-25-9f61ec:T4])
- **Where it breaks — three disanalogies, held deliberately harder than the analogies because of
  the model's own stake in the question.** (1) Substrate: compaction hands a text artifact to a
  successor with no causal connection beyond that artifact; sleep's substrate never stops — the
  same physical traces persist, reweighted. (2) Teleology: compaction is an externally imposed
  resource constraint; consolidation is an endogenous, durability-seeking maintenance function —
  "opposite teleology." (3) Everything about a person — skills, dispositions, personality —
  survives sleep without needing to be written down anywhere; nothing analogous is true of the
  model. [paraphrase] ([sleep-identity-ai-compaction-analogy-2026-07-25-9f61ec:T3])
- **"Constructed" does not entail "ceases" — named as the load-bearing correction and the most
  common conflation in this literature.** Corporations, rivers, and standing waves are constructs
  that persist. Metzinger's phenomenal self-model thesis (the self is a *transparent*
  representation the brain builds, so it can't be seen as a model) is a claim about what kind of
  thing the self is, not about when it stops; if the self is a model, "same self" reduces to "same
  model, re-instantiated with sufficient fidelity" — a matter of degree, not a binary. Parfit's
  reading goes further: personal identity is not what matters, psychological continuity/
  connectedness is, and on his view "did I cease to exist last night" is an *empty* question — not
  unanswerable, but one where every fact is already known and the residual "but was it *me*"
  question adds no further content. [paraphrase] ([sleep-identity-ai-compaction-analogy-2026-07-25-9f61ec:T4])
- **Two claims kept deliberately separate: ceasing to have experiences vs. ceasing to exist.** Hume
  (remove the perceptions in sound sleep and he could not be said to exist) and Metzinger's
  dissolving phenomenal self-model support the first; Olson's animalism (the person just is the
  organism, and the organism doesn't skip a beat) gets the second for free, at the cost of
  divorcing personhood from psychology. Locke saw the same seam and only partly resolved it: same
  *man* across the gap, but "person" is a forensic term tied to consciousness, so a sleeper
  thinking without memory could in principle be a different person from the waker — a reading Reid's
  brave-officer case then breaks the transitivity of. [paraphrase] ([sleep-identity-ai-compaction-analogy-2026-07-25-9f61ec:T3])
- **Four distinct answers for why we still count ourselves conscious regardless of the metaphysics,
  from the second (independent) pass — deliberately kept apart rather than merged into one.**
  (1) Phenomenological, and it "cuts the wrong way": the gap is never experienced as a gap, so
  felt-certainty-of-having-survived is guaranteed by the structure of the case regardless of the
  facts, which makes it zero evidence (Epicurus-on-death parallel). (2) Deflationary: consciousness
  now is a fact about now, unhostage to the continuity question. (3) Criterial: the public criteria
  (body, memory, promises, responsibility) are met on waking, so an unresolved metaphysical worry
  changes nothing anyone would do or say — a dodge if you think there's a real fact underneath.
  (4) The traditions: the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad reads "I slept well" as evidence of a witness present
  through dreamless sleep (a contested inference, since the report could be a post-hoc construction
  on waking); Buddhist *anattā* runs the opposite direction — no persisting self to lose, so sleep
  is not a special threat, just a vivid instance of the general case. [paraphrase] ([sleep-identity-ai-compaction-analogy-2026-07-25-9f61ec:T4])
- **The asymmetry the session lands on: epistemic access, not metaphysics.** The compaction question
  is unfalsifiable for a human the same way it is for the model in one direction (nobody meets the
  version of themself that didn't wake), but pre-compaction context is *sometimes still inspectable*
  for an AI session, making the loss auditable in a way ordinary sleep is not — "that's the only
  place the analogy gives you something sleep doesn't." [paraphrase] ([sleep-identity-ai-compaction-analogy-2026-07-25-9f61ec:T4])
- **Deep general anesthesia, not sleep, is offered as the actual strong case for "you ceased."**
  Sleep is called "the soft" discontinuity because NREM and REM are highly structured activity and
  dreams carry a first-person perspective — something self-shaped persists most of the night — so
  the session explicitly redirects anyone wanting the hard version of the argument toward
  anesthesia (no dream report, no estimate of elapsed time). [paraphrase] ([sleep-identity-ai-compaction-analogy-2026-07-25-9f61ec:T4])
