## Summary

Short session (3.3K chars, 2026-03-29). Jon asked Claude to assess the Stylomantic project across three frames: applying skills at new scale, learning to use AI, and maybe improving AI for everyone. Session provides the clearest statement of the project's triple motivation and an honest assessment of where the obsession lives.

## Key Claims

- Three framings of Stylomantic: (1) applying actuarial statistical discipline at ML scale — described as "the most defensible part, genuinely real"; (2) learning to use AI well — where "some of the obsession lives," the project as a long runway for learning Claude Code, Ollama, logprob APIs; (3) improving AI for everyone — where Claude pushed back hardest, requires v0.1 results to be strong and null treated as null. ([stylomantic-motivations-2026-03-29-4b9fcc:T2])
- Jon's actuarial training is doing real work on Stylomantic: pre-registration, BH correction, temporal splits, confound identification, power analysis before collection — "not table stakes in ML." Most ML practitioners would not apply these. ([stylomantic-motivations-2026-03-29-4b9fcc:T2])
- The "Skittles problem" and EOS confound were identified and built around — specific domain errors that required actuarial framing to catch. ([stylomantic-motivations-2026-03-29-4b9fcc:T2])
- "The outside-in framing you've developed is intellectually honest for what's achievable without internals access." Jon framed the logprob layer correctly as outside-in, which is rare in the space. ([stylomantic-motivations-2026-03-29-4b9fcc:T2])
- The risk: elaborateness of design becomes a substitute for starting data collection. The research arc section is more expansive than the power analysis section, suggesting imagination running ahead of evidence. ([stylomantic-motivations-2026-03-29-4b9fcc:T2])
