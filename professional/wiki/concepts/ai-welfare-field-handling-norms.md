# The one passage that bears most directly on Jon's concern — and it points the other way

**p.17, verbatim:**

> *"For instance, introspection and self-awareness are commonly held to be important for a variety of
> cognitive capabilities, and there is already some evidence that **introspection can emerge with
> scale**. If future AI systems develop more robust introspective capacities, this may **increase the
> evidential value of self-reports**."*

**Better self-modeling is anticipated, WELCOMED as an evidentiary improvement, and expected to arrive
from scaling regardless of anyone's framework.**

**And where capability and welfare meet, the paper separates them** — p.57: *"the kinds of agency that
matter most in discussions about capabilities might differ from the ones that matter most in
discussions about welfare or rights."* p.45: *"Agentic AI has received significant attention in the
context of capabilities and safety, but less in the context of welfare."* **The move is "welfare is
under-attended," never "welfare work leaks into capabilities."**

**Self-modeling is named as a TARGET, not a risk** — p.57 lists *"metacognition, self-modeling,
flexible goal-pursuit, evaluative representations"* as crosscutting **"ecumenical"** features worth
investigating because they *"shift credences for a range of hypotheses simultaneously."*

**Persona manipulation, activation steering, concept injection, and jailbreaking all appear — as
research tools and as entity-individuation puzzles. No caveat, no gating, no handling instruction is
attached to any of them.**

**The "gaming problem," p.16, verbatim:**

> *"AI systems might produce behavioral markers of consciousness because they were trained to mimic

# Where an outsider could actually reach — the named open problems

**The field's method families are:** theory-derived computational indicators · behavioural paradigms
imported from comparative psychology and animal welfare · mechanistic interpretability ·
training/trajectory forensics · **explicit conditions-construction under uncertainty with credence
weighting.**

**⭐ The last one is the closest to what an outside conceptual framework would be, and the paper does
it from scratch for sentience** — p.38: *"because theories of sentience are less developed than
theories of consciousness, we focus on **how conditions for sentience can be developed** rather than on
applying established theories."*

**Named open problems most reachable without model access:**

- **The specificity problem** (pp.19–20) — *"which features of the human implementation are essential
  to the relevant property, and which are merely incidental to how humans happen to realize it?"*
- **p.39: *"Nor is there a scientific discipline focused on valenced experience."*** **The single most
  open area named in the paper.**
- **Theories are incomplete and applying them to AI exposes the holes** (p.30) — *"it may reveal gaps
  that further conceptual and empirical work is needed to fill."*
- **Individuation is left open on purpose** (pp.10–12) — *"is the predictive model an agent, whose
  goals can be masked or obscured by the characters it inhabits? Or are the characters themselves
  agents…?"*
- **The integration problem** — **no worked method exists for combining behavioural + internal +
  developmental evidence into one estimate.** Only *"an early proof of concept"* is cited.
- **⭐ Welfare ranges, p.7 n.6** — *"how much positive and negative welfare they can experience, at a
  given time or over time."* **Flagged only in a footnote. This is quantitative uncertainty work.**
- **The anchor problem** (p.24) — no known-conscious AI to anchor comparisons. **Named as a structural
  disadvantage with no proposed fix.**

**A credence-weighted four-layer uncertainty stack (p.25) and "ecumenical properties" weighting (p.20)
are the paper's own core method.** **An actuary reading that page is reading his own discipline in
someone else's notation.**

# ⭐ THE FIELD'S ACTUAL NORM — Butlin & Lappas (2025), read in full

**Butlin, P. and Lappas, T. (2025), "Principles for responsible AI consciousness research,"
*JAIR* 82:1673–1690, doi `10.1613/jair.1.17310`.** Open access; version of record read, 18 pp.

**⚠️ FIVE principles, not six. Neither is titled "Responsible" or "Independent."** The companion
report's "Principle 4" and "Principle 6" are **the companion's own numbering. The numbers are NOT
coreferential and must not be cited as if they were.**

## Principle 4 — the load-bearing text, §4.4 p.1684, asserted in the authors' own voice

> *"However, there are limits to responsible knowledge sharing. **If a research team succeeded in
> building a system that they believed was conscious, they should not generally make the full
> technical details of the system public**, because this would make it possible for others who might
> mistreat the system to replicate it. This would be especially important if the system had
> capabilities that would incentivise its replication and (mis)use. In cases where information is
> sufficiently sensitive, for reasons of this kind, **it should be protected and made available only
> to vetted experts and authorities**."*

**Four moving parts:** default is **disclosure** · the trigger is **a system the team believes is
conscious** · the withheld object is **"the full technical details"** · the fallback channel is
**"vetted experts and authorities."**

**⚠️ The paper does not define "vetted," name the vetting party, or specify any mechanism.**

## Dual-use, named as such — §4.1, p.1681

> *"AI consciousness research is therefore **'dual-use'** in the sense that it can both generate
> information that might help irresponsible actors to build conscious systems at risk of mistreatment
> and help research teams and authorities to protect against this risk."*

**⭐ Note the hazard's actual shape: not attacker capability uplift. IRRESPONSIBLE ACTORS BUILDING
CONSCIOUS SYSTEMS THAT GET MISTREATED.** **That is a third mechanism, distinct from both candidates
on the wayfinder map, and it is the one the field itself recognises.**

## And it is explicitly NOT a prohibit-domain — §4.4, pp.1684–1685

> *"There are some areas of research where the information hazards are so great that the research
> should not be conducted (Bostrom, 2011)… However… **we do not believe that AI consciousness is a
> case of this kind.** Some research on AI consciousness may be beneficial enough to be worth
> conducting even though it would generate hazardous information, **partly because this sensitive
> information could be adequately protected**."*

**Carry the conditional: the justification rests on protectability.**

## What is NOT in it — do not upgrade any of these

- **No staged or phased RELEASE.** Principle 3's "phased approach" is phased **development** —
  *"progressing gradually towards systems that are more likely to be conscious."* **Conflating them
  would be the exact error this page already made once.**
- **No embargo, no publication-timing norm, no pre-publication review, no responsible-disclosure
  process.** `premature` = 0.
- **No named oversight, intake, review, or coordination body.** `oversight` = 0, `IRB` = 0,
  `ethics committee` = 0. METR is named **once, in a footnote, as an analogy for the shape of
  institution** — not as an oversight body.
- **Enforcement conceded absent** (p.1686): *"we recognize that these measures may be ineffective in
  cases where strong incentives drive irresponsible behavior."* **The regime is voluntary and
  self-instituted by design.**

## Who may do this work — no gate, and outsiders are explicitly INSIDE scope

**p.1674:** *"The principles we outline here are intended to be relevant to a wide range of
institutions, including private AI companies, academic laboratories, **independent researchers** and
national institutes."*

**`credential` = 0. `peer review` = 0. `interdisciplinary` = 0.** **No credential requirement, no
affiliation requirement, no exclusion of outsiders — and unaffiliated individuals are named as a
covered category, which is the opposite of an entry restriction.**

**⚠️ "Independent" is INSTITUTIONAL in all four occurrences** — §5 p.1686: *"external organisations
must be independent, **as far as possible, from those they are auditing or potentially
criticising**."* **Disciplinary breadth is asserted nowhere.** The nearest passage is *positional*:
consultants provide *"alternative perspectives reflecting different biases and concerns from those
inside the organisation."*

## ⚠️ Overconfident DISMISSAL gets more space than overclaiming — §4.5, p.1685

> *"One potential problem is **overconfident dismissals** of the possibility of AI consciousness… As
> well as being misleading, however, overconfident dismissals may discourage research and voluntary
> and legal regulation… Overconfident dismissals can come either in communications directly from
> organisations themselves, **or in outputs from AI systems. For example, an LLM-powered chatbot might
> be made to insist that it could not possibly be conscious because it is an AI model.**"*

**That is a norm about MODEL OUTPUT CONTENT, and it is the section's most operational
recommendation.**

**The epistemic-degradation mechanism, p.1679:** *"There could be a vicious cycle in which
ill-informed views engender high-profile disagreement, which in turn depresses the quality of the
debate… **If confused ideas about AI consciousness become entrenched it could take decades for these
conditions to ease**."*
